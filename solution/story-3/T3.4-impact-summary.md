## 1. Executive Summary

T3.4 consolidates the completed T3.2 and T3.3 measurements. It does not introduce another Docker or application-code change.

Story 3 produced one validated keep-now change and one carry-forward prototype:

- **Keep now:** the targeted `.dockerignore`, which reduced Docker build context from `191.27MB` to `189B` while preserving the required runtime artefacts. The targeted `.dockerignore` was validated successfully in the SNS repository. For durable reuse and consistency across command-adaptor repositories, `.dockerignore` ownership and distribution should be considered through RepoSync. RepoSync adoption is a recommended follow-up. Final image size remained unchanged and no cold-build or CI saving is claimed.
- **Carry forward:** the layer-order prototype, which reduced local same-daemon warm-cache rebuild time after a real JAR content change from `75.82-77.90s` to `4.62-5.08s`. It is not approved for the production Dockerfile until RepoSync/platform ownership and CI applicability are confirmed.

### Measurement boundaries

The T2.3 no-change warm build (`real 0m0.851s`) and the T3.3 JAR-change warm rebuilds measure different scenarios. The no-change value shows a build where all relevant layers are already cached. T3.3 tests whether the expensive setup layer remains reusable after the application JAR content changes. These values must not be compared as before and after measurements.

The T3.2 image was `906MB`. T3.3 measured both the current Dockerfile and the prototype at `875MB` within the same later session. The cross-session difference reflects base-image/session variance, not the layer-order change. The valid T3.3 image-size comparison is `875MB` versus `875MB`; therefore no image-size reduction is claimed.

The T3.3 result depends on a persistent cache in the same local Docker daemon. Drone/DIND cache reuse between CI runs was not measured, so the local speed-up must not be translated into a CI saving.

## 3. T3.2 Decision - Keep the Targeted `.dockerignore`

**Decision:** Keep the targeted `.dockerignore`.

It safely reduces Docker build context from `191.27MB` to `189B` while preserving `cmd-adaptor-sns-exec.jar` and `opentelemetry-javaagent.jar` in the final image. Final image size remained `906MB`, and cold-build time remained broadly similar at `real 78.14s`. The targeted `.dockerignore` was validated successfully in the SNS repository. For durable reuse and consistency across command-adaptor repositories, `.dockerignore` ownership and distribution should be considered through RepoSync. RepoSync adoption is a recommended follow-up.

The measured benefit is build-context reduction. This decision does not claim an image-size, cold-build-time or CI improvement. If `.dockerignore` is generated or overwritten by RepoSync, the validated content should be carried through the confirmed central ownership route rather than treated as a durable repo-local change.

## 4. T3.3 Decision - Carry Forward the Layer-Order Prototype

**Decision:** Carry forward the layer-order prototype as a candidate for RepoSync/platform ownership discussion.

The prototype produced a reproducible local same-daemon warm-cache improvement after a real JAR content change: `77.90s` to `5.08s` in run 1 and `75.82s` to `4.62s` in run 2. It achieves this by keeping the expensive `yum`/`envconsul` setup layer before the frequently changing application artefacts, allowing that setup layer to remain cached.

The prototype should not be adopted into the production Dockerfile until RepoSync/platform ownership and CI applicability are confirmed. The production Dockerfile remains unchanged, and no adaptor-family rollout is recommended from this local SNS measurement alone.
